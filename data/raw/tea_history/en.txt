According to a Chinese legend, tea was discovered almost five thousand years ago by the emperor Shennong. One day, while he was resting under a tree, a few leaves fell into a pot of boiling water. The emperor tasted the drink and found it refreshing. Whether or not the story is true, tea has been part of Chinese culture for thousands of years.

During the Tang dynasty, a scholar named Lu Yu wrote "The Classic of Tea", the first book entirely devoted to this drink. In it, he described how to grow the plant, how to process the leaves and even which kind of water is best for brewing. For Lu Yu, drinking tea was not a habit but an art.

Tea reached Europe in the seventeenth century. At first it was an expensive luxury that only the rich could afford. In Britain, the custom of afternoon tea appeared in the nineteenth century and soon became a national tradition.

Russia discovered tea by land rather than by sea. Caravans of camels carried boxes of tea across Mongolia and Siberia, and the journey could take more than a year. That is why tea in Russia was associated with the samovar, long conversations and a slow pace of life.

Today tea is the most popular drink in the world after water. Every day people drink billions of cups of it. Each culture, however, has its own way of drinking it: with milk, with lemon, with jam or with nothing at all.
